# UNTITLED EXTRACTION SHOOTER

## PvEvP Extraction Shooter Built Around Choice, Weather, Physical Loot, and Meaningful Death

> *No match timer. No revive spam. No forced battle royale circle. Just you, your squad, the world, the weather, your loot, and the choices you make.*

---

# About The Game

This game is a **PvEvP extraction shooter** with a stronger focus on **PvE, world danger, survival pressure, and player choice** than pure PvP hunting.

It is not trying to be the perfect extraction shooter for everyone.

It is built around one core idea:

> **The game should not force decisions. It should create situations where players make choices.**

Instead of a strict match timer, the pressure comes from the world itself:

* changing weather
* shifting extraction options
* limited reinforcements
* physical loot handling
* enemy players
* dangerous PvE threats
* body recovery
* greed
* trust
* betrayal

The game is not as realistic and slow as *Escape from Tarkov*. Movement should feel smoother and more fun, closer to *Call of Duty*, but toned down so it still fits an extraction shooter.

You can slide, dive, move quickly, fight smoothly, and play aggressively, but the game still keeps the tension of extraction.

---

# Core Identity

This game is about:

* PvEvP extraction gameplay
* Smooth, responsive movement
* No forced match timer
* Dynamic weather-based extractions
* Limited team-based reinforcements
* Physical loot and body interaction
* Flexible gadget-based roles
* Proximity chat
* Trust and betrayal
* Player-made stories

The world is not just a background.

The world is what creates pressure.

---

# Core Gameplay Loop

1. Enter an active raid with your squad.
2. Explore the map, fight PvE enemies, gather loot, and complete objectives.
3. Encounter other players through combat, negotiation, or uneasy cooperation.
4. Weather changes the available extraction options.
5. Players collect loot physically through backpacks, bodies, and carried containers.
6. If a teammate dies, the squad must reach a reinforcement station to bring them back.
7. If the full squad dies, the squad is out of that lobby permanently.
8. Players choose whether to:

   * extract themselves
   * extract loot only
   * recover a teammate’s body
   * recover an enemy’s body
   * stay longer for more rewards
9. The raid continues without a global timer.

The game does not say:

> “Leave now or die because the timer ends.”

Instead, it creates situations like:

> “The storm just disabled helicopters.”
> “The lower valley is flooding.”
> “Boat extracts are active now.”
> “You have one reinforcement left.”
> “Your teammate’s body is still out there.”
> “You can send the loot out, but you’ll lose the backpack.”
> “Do you stay or leave?”

That is the heart of the game.

---

# PvEvP Focus

The game is technically PvP and PvE, but the focus leans more toward **PvE-driven extraction chaos**.

Other players are dangerous, but they are not the only threat.

The world itself should be dangerous enough that players are not always just hunting each other.

PvE threats could include:

* hostile factions
* monsters or infected
* wildlife
* patrols
* armored enemies
* environmental hazards
* weather-related danger
* special roaming threats

The PvE should matter.

It should weaken players, force movement, create noise, block routes, and interrupt fights.

The ideal feeling is:

> **The world is the main enemy. Other players are the unpredictable wildcard.**

---

# Movement

Movement should feel smooth, responsive, and satisfying.

Not ultra-realistic.

Not clunky.

Not Tarkov-style heavy movement.

More like a toned-down version of *Call of Duty*.

Default movement abilities could include:

* sliding
* diving
* sprinting
* mantling
* crouching
* quick repositioning
* smooth aiming transitions

The goal is to make fights feel fun and skillful without turning the game into a full arcade movement shooter.

Players should be able to make plays, escape bad situations, dive into cover, slide across openings, and move naturally through the environment.

But movement should still have enough weight that carrying bodies, carrying extra backpacks, or being overloaded changes how you play.

---

# No Global Match Timer

One of the biggest design choices is that the game has **no traditional raid timer**.

There is no giant countdown forcing everyone to leave.

This makes the game feel more natural.

Instead of artificial pressure, the game uses:

* weather
* limited reinforcements
* changing extractions
* player density
* PvE escalation
* loot greed
* body recovery
* limited supplies
* extraction risk

The idea is not that players can safely camp forever.

The idea is that the pressure should come from the situation, not from a random clock.

---

# Active Lobbies

Because there is no timer, players can potentially join active lobbies.

The map is not simply a closed match that starts and ends for everyone at the same time.

Players may enter a raid already in progress.

However, to avoid unfairness, the game could use soft matchmaking.

That means when you join, you might be placed into a lobby with:

* similar weather
* similar world state
* similar danger level
* available player slots

It may feel like the same ongoing world, even if matchmaking is quietly balancing things behind the scenes.

There could technically be a chance that after dying or leaving, you end up in the same lobby again, but there should be no easy way to know or abuse it.

---

# Squad Size

Maximum squad size should probably be **trios**.

Trios are a good balance because:

* fights stay readable
* teamwork matters
* solos and duos still have a chance
* squads are not too overwhelming
* communication stays manageable

The game should support:

* solo
* duo
* trio

But trios would be the maximum.

---

# Proximity Chat

Proximity chat is essential.

The game should allow players to talk to nearby enemies, strangers, and temporary allies.

This supports:

* negotiation
* betrayal
* begging
* fake surrender
* temporary cooperation
* body recovery promises
* intimidation
* funny moments
* social chaos

Because of the body extraction system, proximity chat becomes even more important.

You could meet an enemy, accidentally kill them, fail to save them, and then promise:

> “I’ll get your body out.”

That kind of moment is what makes the game special.

---

# Weapon Customization

Weapon customization should be broad and fun, but not completely stupid.

The idea is:

> **Freedom within believable limits.**

Any weapon should support many attachment combinations, especially:

* scopes
* red dots
* grips
* lasers
* flashlights
* suppressors
* muzzle devices
* magazines
* stocks

But not absurd combinations like putting a .50 cal sniper barrel on an M4.

The goal is to allow creativity and personal builds without destroying the identity of each weapon.

Players should be able to make weapons for:

* close quarters
* stealth
* recoil control
* long range
* night fighting
* heavy PvE fighting
* lightweight scouting

Weapon builds should feel personal and fun.

---

# Gadgets Instead of Fixed Classes

The game should not have strict hero classes.

Instead, roles are created through gadgets.

A player should be able to build weird but useful combinations like:

> **Recon healer.**

That means you are not locked into:

* medic
* assault
* recon
* support

Instead, you choose gadgets that define what you can do.

Example gadgets could include:

* recon drone
* healing kit
* body scanner
* reinforcement beacon
* smoke launcher
* portable radar
* loot tracker
* weather scanner
* armor repair tool
* extraction flare
* body drag harness
* silent movement tool
* backpack beacon

This gives players freedom.

You can build your own role instead of being forced into one.

---

# Death System

The game should avoid traditional DBNO revive spam.

In many shooters, a player can be downed, revived, downed again, revived again, and keep going forever.

This game should not work like that.

When you die, you actually die.

But death does not always mean you are done forever.

Instead of solo redeploying randomly, the game uses a **team-based reinforcement system**.

---

# Reinforcement System

The chosen system is:

> **Option one: reinforcement stations only.**

There is no random solo redeploy.

If a player dies, their squad must actively bring them back.

To do that, the surviving squad members must reach a reinforcement station or similar location.

Reinforcements are limited, probably to **three total uses**.

This replaces the normal DBNO system completely.

A dead teammate cannot just randomly spawn behind the enemies with a full kit and get revenge.

The squad has to:

* survive
* move to a station
* secure the area
* spend a reinforcement use
* bring the teammate back

This makes bringing someone back a real objective.

---

# Full Squad Wipe

If the full squad dies, they cannot rejoin that lobby.

This is the hard rule that gives the game stakes.

As long as at least one squad member is alive, the team still has a chance.

But if everyone dies, the run is over.

That means every late-game fight becomes intense.

If your squad has one reinforcement left and only one player alive, the situation becomes serious without needing a timer.

---

# Reinforcement Balance

Reinforcement stations should not feel free or safe.

They could:

* take time to activate
* make noise
* send a signal
* attract PvE enemies
* be visible to nearby players
* require money or a consumable
* have limited uses
* be disabled by weather or power loss

Calling reinforcements should feel like a choice.

You are saving your teammate, but you are also exposing yourself.

---

# Physical Bodies

Bodies are not just loot containers.

Bodies are physical objects in the world.

Players can:

* drag bodies
* carry bodies
* move downed or dead players
* hide bodies
* extract bodies
* steal enemy bodies
* protect teammate bodies

This creates a lot of emergent gameplay.

A body is:

* loot
* evidence
* an objective
* a bargaining chip
* a promise
* a risk

---

# Dragging Bodies

Players can drag human bodies across the ground.

Dragging is slower than normal movement and makes the player vulnerable.

While dragging, you might:

* move slowly
* make noise
* have limited weapon control
* consume stamina
* expose yourself to enemies

Dragging is useful when:

* moving a teammate’s body to safety
* hiding enemy loot
* recovering someone for extraction
* pulling a body out of fire
* denying enemies access to their gear

---

# Carrying Bodies Over the Shoulder

Players may also be able to carry a body over their shoulders.

This is stronger than dragging but more risky.

While carrying a body, the player could:

* move faster than dragging but slower than normal
* lose access to primary weapons
* maybe only use a pistol
* consume stamina quickly
* be very visible
* be unable to sprint properly

This allows funny, chaotic, and tactical moments.

You could literally steal an enemy’s body and hide it somewhere.

Yes, it could be annoying.

But that is part of the game.

The important thing is that it has friction.

If you want to steal a body, you must accept the risk of being slow, loud, and vulnerable.

---

# Body Extraction

One of the most unique systems is body extraction.

If you bring a player’s body to an extraction, the player who owns that body gets their loot back.

This applies even if they are dead and no longer in the match.

For example:

You meet an enemy through proximity chat.

Something goes wrong.

They die.

Maybe you killed them by accident.

Maybe you tried to help but did not have the right item.

Maybe they were downed and you could not save them in time.

Instead of just stealing everything, you can choose to carry their body to extraction.

If you successfully extract their body, they receive their loot.

Maybe they get a pop-up:

> “Your body was recovered. Your equipment has been returned.”

This creates a rare kind of social gameplay.

You can save someone’s outcome without saving their life.

---

# Why Body Extraction Matters

Body extraction creates:

* trust
* betrayal
* mercy
* social contracts
* player stories
* rescue missions
* emotional stakes

It allows situations like:

> “I can’t revive you, but I’ll get your body out.”

That is much more interesting than a normal revive button.

It also gives players something meaningful to do after a fight besides looting and leaving.

---

# Loot Extraction Without Player Extraction

Another major system is the ability to extract loot without extracting yourself.

You could bring a backpack full of loot to:

* a helicopter
* a boat
* a car
* an extraction crate
* a cargo drone
* a supply pickup

Then you throw the backpack inside, pay a fee, and the loot gets sent home.

But you stay in the raid.

This is powerful, but it is still a choice.

You secure your loot, but you lose the backpack.

That means you either:

* sacrifice your own backpack
* bring an extra backpack
* steal someone else’s backpack
* find another later

This creates a real tradeoff.

---

# Loot Extraction Tax

Loot extraction should probably cost money.

This could be:

* a flat fee
* a percentage of the loot value
* a service cost based on backpack size
* a faction fee
* a bribe
* a limited-use extraction ticket

This keeps the system from being too free.

It is strong, maybe even OP, but it has cost and risk.

---

# Backpack Requirement

To extract loot separately, the loot must be inside a backpack or similar container.

You cannot just magically send inventory away.

This makes backpacks physically important.

A backpack is not just UI space.

It is a container in the world.

You may choose to:

* keep your backpack
* send it away
* carry a second one
* steal one
* hide one
* fill one and come back later

---

# Carrying Extra Backpacks

Players can pick up an extra backpack, fill it with loot, and carry it in their hand.

This gives more loot capacity, but comes with a major downside.

While carrying a backpack in one hand, you may only be able to use:

* a pistol
* a small SMG
* one-handed tools
* maybe no weapon at all depending on balance

You are more vulnerable.

You are visibly carrying extra loot.

Other players can see that you are probably valuable.

This turns greed into a physical risk.

You are not just heavy in a menu.

You are obviously carrying something worth stealing.

---

# Extra Backpack Gameplay

Carrying an extra backpack creates choices like:

* Do I drop the bag and fight?
* Do I run with only a pistol?
* Do I hide the bag before pushing?
* Do I send the bag out and keep playing?
* Do I give the bag to a teammate?
* Do I use the bag as bait?

This is exactly the game’s philosophy:

> It is not a forced decision.
> It is a choice created by the situation.

---

# Inventory Philosophy

The inventory system needs careful thought.

A pure weight-based system can be annoying because items become too heavy and players feel punished for looting.

A pure slot-based system can feel gamey and awkward.

The best version may be a hybrid.

Possible inventory design:

* slot-based backpack space for clarity
* soft weight thresholds for movement penalties
* physical carried objects for extra loot
* backpacks as real containers
* heavy items slowing the player but not making the game miserable

The goal is to make looting meaningful without turning it into suffering.

---

# Armor System

Armor is still undecided.

The game could have armor, but it should not make players feel like bullet sponges.

A good armor system might include:

* simple armor tiers
* durability
* limited repair
* armor plates
* no overly complex plate levels
* clear weak points
* meaningful but not ridiculous protection

Armor should help, but it should not remove the danger of getting shot.

---

# Healing System

The healing system should be deeper than simple instant healing, but not overly complicated.

The game probably should not have a super complex medical system.

Possible conditions:

* bleeding
* broken bone
* low health
* maybe concussion or stamina damage

Healing items could include:

* bandage
* medkit
* splint
* painkiller
* trauma kit

The goal is:

* enough depth to make injuries matter
* not so much depth that healing becomes a chore

---

# Dynamic Weather System

Weather is a core feature.

It does not just change visuals.

It changes gameplay.

Weather affects:

* visibility
* sound
* movement
* extraction availability
* PvE behavior
* routes
* flooding
* vehicles
* helicopters
* boats
* player decisions

Weather is one of the main ways the game creates pressure without using a timer.

---

# Weather-Based Extractions

Dynamic extractions can be controlled by weather.

Examples:

## Heavy Rain

Heavy rain reduces visibility and makes sound harder to read.

It may cause flooding in lower areas.

Effects:

* low-ground helicopter extracts become unavailable
* water levels rise
* boat extracts become more common
* muddy terrain slows movement
* rivers become stronger
* some tunnels or roads flood

## Flooding

Flooding changes the map physically.

Effects:

* lower roads blocked
* underground routes closed
* boats can reach new areas
* certain extraction points become dangerous
* loot areas may become unreachable
* players are forced to higher ground

## Heavy Wind

Strong wind can disable helicopters.

Effects:

* helicopter extractions temporarily unavailable
* parachute or rope insertions disabled
* long-range shooting becomes harder
* trees, debris, or environmental hazards become active
* boat or ground vehicle extracts become more valuable

## Storm

A major storm can make the whole map more chaotic.

Effects:

* poor visibility
* loud thunder masking movement
* electronic gadgets less reliable
* drones harder to use
* helicopters unavailable
* PvE enemies behave differently

## Fog

Fog creates stealth and ambush gameplay.

Effects:

* long-range visibility reduced
* snipers weaker
* close-range fights more common
* recon gadgets become stronger
* extraction camping becomes riskier because players can approach unseen

---

# Extraction Types

The game can have multiple extraction types.

## Helicopter Extraction

Fast and useful, but weather-dependent.

Can be disabled by:

* heavy wind
* storms
* low visibility
* flooding in landing zones

Helicopters are loud and obvious.

They can extract:

* players
* backpacks
* bodies

## Boat Extraction

Useful during flooding and heavy rain.

Boats may become available when water levels rise.

They can access areas helicopters cannot.

They may be slower but safer in certain weather.

## Vehicle Extraction

Ground vehicles can be used when air extraction is not possible.

They may require roads to be clear.

Floods, mud, enemies, or roadblocks can disable them.

## Cargo Extraction

Used for loot-only extraction.

Players send backpacks, containers, or valuable items out without leaving.

---

# Extraction Windows

Extractions do not need to be permanently active.

They could appear in windows.

For example:

* helicopter available for 3 minutes
* boat arrives when flood level rises
* truck convoy passes through at certain times
* cargo drone only appears after being called

This creates urgency without a global timer.

---

# Loot-Only Extraction Example

A player finds rare loot.

They do not want to leave yet.

They reach a boat extraction.

They throw a backpack into the boat and pay the fee.

The boat leaves with the loot.

The player stays behind.

Now they have:

* secured part of their reward
* lost a backpack
* reduced their carrying capacity
* gained freedom to take more risks

This is a strong choice.

---

# Body Recovery Example

A teammate dies in a fight.

The squad cannot reinforce them yet.

One player drags the body into a nearby building.

Another player covers the street.

Later, the squad reaches a reinforcement station and brings the teammate back.

The returning teammate travels back to the body and recovers their loot.

This creates a natural recovery mission.

---

# Enemy Body Extraction Example

You meet an enemy solo player.

You talk through proximity chat.

They get caught in a fight and die.

You could steal everything.

Instead, you carry their body to extraction.

When the body extracts, that player gets their gear back.

After the match, they receive a message that their body was recovered.

This creates a moment of trust in a genre usually built on paranoia.

---

# Stealing Bodies Example

You wipe an enemy squad.

Instead of just looting them, you carry their bodies away and hide them.

Their teammate returns through reinforcements and cannot find the bodies.

This is annoying, funny, and memorable.

It should be allowed because it creates stories.

But it must be balanced by making body carrying risky.

---

# Player Choice Philosophy

The whole game is based on choices, not forced decisions.

A forced decision is:

> “The timer is ending, so I must leave.”

A choice is:

> “The storm disabled helicopters, my teammate is dead, I have one reinforcement left, and I’m carrying a backpack full of loot. Do I go to the boat, call reinforcement, extract the bag, or keep pushing?”

That is the difference.

The game should create pressure naturally.

---

# Risk Should Be Physical

In this game, risk is visible.

Examples:

* carrying a body makes you slow
* carrying a backpack limits your weapon
* calling reinforcements makes noise
* extracting loot costs your backpack
* dragging a body exposes you
* staying longer increases danger
* weather changes routes

The player should feel risk through actions, not just numbers in a menu.

# Match Flow Example

## Start

A trio enters the map during light rain.

They have basic gear, one recon gadget, one healing gadget, and one smoke gadget.

They move toward an industrial area for loot.

## First Contact

They fight PvE enemies and gather valuable items.

One player finds an extra backpack and fills it with loot.

Now they can only use a pistol while carrying it.

## Weather Shift

Rain becomes heavier.

Lower roads begin flooding.

Helicopter extraction near the valley shuts down.

Boat extraction becomes available near the lake.

## Player Encounter

The squad meets another duo.

They talk through proximity chat.

Nobody trusts each other fully.

A PvE patrol interrupts the conversation.

One enemy player dies.

The surviving enemy asks:

> “Please get his body out.”

Now the squad has a choice.

## Body Recovery

One player carries the enemy body.

Another carries the loot backpack.

The third covers.

They are slow, vulnerable, and valuable.

## Ambush

Another squad sees them moving.

They attack because the group clearly has loot and a body.

A fight breaks out.

One teammate dies.

## Reinforcement Choice

The squad could push to boat extraction now, but they have a dead teammate.

They decide to go to a reinforcement station first.

They use one of their three reinforcement charges.

The teammate comes back.

## Loot Extraction

The squad reaches the boat.

They throw the loot backpack into the boat and pay the extraction fee.

The loot is safe, but the player loses the extra backpack.

## Body Extraction

They also load the enemy body onto the boat.

The dead enemy player receives their gear back.

## Continue or Leave

Now the squad can leave, but they have secured some loot already.

They choose to stay longer.

The storm gets worse.

Helicopters are still disabled.

They have two reinforcements left.

The raid continues.

# Why This Game Is Different

Most extraction shooters are built around:

* timers
* camping extracts
* harsh inventory
* revive spam or permadeath
* fixed extraction points
* rigid metas

This game is different because it focuses on:

* dynamic weather
* physical loot
* body logistics
* team-based reinforcement
* social trust
* flexible gadgets
* no forced timer
* player-created tension

It is still an extraction shooter, but the tension comes from systems interacting.

Not from a circle.

Not from a timer.

Not from infinite revives.

---

# Main Design Pillars

## 1. Choice Over Forced Decisions

The game creates situations, but the player chooses what to do.

## 2. Physical Risk

Loot, bodies, backpacks, and reinforcements exist in the world physically.

## 3. Weather Controls the World

Weather changes extraction, routes, visibility, and strategy.

## 4. Death Matters

No revive spam. Death is real, but squads can call limited reinforcements.

## 5. Social Gameplay Matters

Proximity chat, body extraction, and promises create human moments.

## 6. Flexible Roles

No locked classes. Gadgets define playstyle.

## 7. PvE Is the Backbone

The world should be dangerous even without enemy players.

# Short Pitch

**Untitled Extraction Shooter** is a PvEvP extraction shooter where the world creates pressure instead of a match timer. Players fight, loot, negotiate, betray, recover bodies, call limited reinforcements, and adapt to weather-driven extraction changes. Loot and bodies are physical objects that can be carried, hidden, stolen, or extracted. Death is meaningful, but not always the end, as surviving squadmates can call reinforcements from dangerous stations. The result is an extraction shooter built around choice, risk, trust, and emergent player stories.

---

# One-Sentence Version

> A PvEvP extraction shooter where weather controls extraction, loot is physical, death creates new objectives, and every risky action is a choice instead of a forced decision.
